# Keyword research

Check the org profile for the qualifier rule, excluded classes, competitor set and current keyword-to-page map. Volume source: see SKILL.md section 4. Without a volume/KD tool, mark volume and KD "unknown" and judge difficulty from the live SERP (who ranks, how big, whether they are niche-specific).

## Keyword mapping (core loop)
1. Define the search intent.
2. Gather realistic keyword variants.
3. Prioritize by intent match, likely value and competition.
4. Map one primary keyword/theme to one URL.
5. Detect and avoid cannibalization (two URLs competing for one term).

## The 7-step workbook method
1. Pull keywords from competitors: top 20 keywords in positions 1-5 and 6-10 per competitor, site structure, keyword gap.
2. Add market keywords. Tag each "Improve Our Ranking" (already ranks) or "Need To Rank".
3. Bucket by page or topic (parent-level).
4. Add data points: search volume, difficulty (KD), intent (Commercial / Informational), current position, assigned status.
5. Sort by volume within each bucket.
6. Remove irrelevant keywords (apply the profile's qualifier and exclusions; confirm borderline ones with the approver).
7. Cluster into Cluster Mapping and the Content Calendar: Primary vs Secondary keywords per page, with SV shown.

## Columns
- Raw Keyword List: Keyword | Monthly Volume | KD | CPC ($) | Search Intent | Funnel Stage (TOF/MOF/BOF) | Category (topic bucket, e.g. "Retention - Ideas").
- Raw Keyword List + Clustering (working version): Page | Keyword | Search Volume | Difficulty | Keyword Intent | Improve vs. Need ("Need To Rank" / "Improve Our Ranking") | Current Keyword Position | Assigned Status.
- Cluster Mapping: per parent topic, the landing page, pillar blog page, supporting posts. Drives the calendar.
- Header counts: Keyword Group | # Keywords | Total Volume | TOF | MOF | BOF.

## Easy-Win rule
KD <= 5 and volume >= 50. Example (PropFuel): "member engagement" 400/4, "member retention" 300/2, "new member onboarding" 100/0.

## Qualifier classification
Apply the org's qualifier rule (the profile states it, e.g. "must contain X"). Classify every keyword as QUALIFIED (contains the qualifier), BROAD (relevant but lacks it), or OTHER.
- Qualified variants usually have higher KD and CPC and need page clusters. Broad variants are easier and cheaper.
- Keep the qualified variant as the focus keyword wherever one exists, even at 10 searches a month. Staff often drop the qualifier, so keep the broad variant as secondary or on the homepage.
- Example (PropFuel): qualifier "association"; "member engagement software" is BROAD, "member engagement software for associations" is QUALIFIED.

## Funnel framework
- TOF (informational): blogs, guides, pillars ("how to reduce churn").
- MOF (problem-aware, comparison): comparison pages, solution guides, case studies ("best X software").
- BOF (high intent, commercial): product and use-case landing pages.
Commercial and transactional terms go on landing pages; informational terms go to blogs.

## Prioritization order
1. Fit with the ICP (qualifier). 2. Intent (BOF money terms first). 3. Difficulty and CPC (CPC is a competitiveness and value signal; a $16 CPC means many ads above organic). 4. Volume.
Keep tiny-volume qualified terms for topical and GEO relevance. Target top 3 for priority keywords. Many low-KD niche terms beat a few head terms.

## Triage colors
| Color | Rule |
|---|---|
| Green keep | Core use-case terms with the qualifier; TOF how-tos |
| Yellow lower priority | Drifts toward generic marketing but the product can serve it |
| Red remove | Excluded classes in the profile (wrong intent, a category you cannot win, agency/service intent) |
| Exception | Integration queries stay in when the profile allows them |
